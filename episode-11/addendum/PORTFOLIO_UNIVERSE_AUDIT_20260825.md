# Episode 11 portfolio universe audit

**Research date:** August 24, 2026

**Portfolio reviewed:** inactive combined Biotech/Semis long-call candidate

**Final inactive review portfolio:** `6a8ce88aeca5765ba9cc1075`

## Bottom line

The portfolio does not contain 23 equally direct beneficiaries of Moderna's personalized cancer therapy. It contains three different things:

1. **Direct therapy exposure:** MRNA, MRK, and BNTX.
2. **Personalized-oncology workflow exposure:** sequencing, tumor profiling, MRD, immune monitoring, sample preparation, and laboratory equipment.
3. **A separate AI-compute regime:** six semiconductor companies that enter only when XBI is below its 200-day moving average.

The third group is a regime diversifier. It is not honest to describe NVDA, ANET, KLAC, TSM, MRVL, or LRCX as material beneficiaries of one personalized mRNA cancer vaccine. The per-patient compute workload is real and economically tiny relative to those companies. Their case depends on the much larger AI infrastructure cycle.

The only holding that fails both the company-quality and thesis-specificity tests is **RXRX**. It is a real AI drug-discovery company with live clinical and partnership programs, yet it is a weak public stock for this book: quarterly revenue was only about $7.7 million in the point-in-time screen, revenue had fallen roughly 60% year over year, quarterly free cash flow was about negative $106 million, and its trailing-year drawdown reached roughly 58%. Its relationship to V940 is general computational drug discovery rather than a dependency of Moderna's therapy. RXRX was removed from the final inactive candidate.

## What I measured

This audit keeps four questions separate:

- **Business quality:** scale, revenue direction, cash generation, balance-sheet capacity, and whether the business is commercial or still dependent on clinical milestones.
- **Thesis transmission:** whether success in individualized neoantigen therapy can create demand for the company's product, data, diagnostic, or service.
- **Stock and option tradability:** median dollar volume plus current long-call expiration availability.
- **Portfolio role:** direct bet, workflow enabler, speculative satellite, or defensive regime asset.

The financial and price snapshot came from NexusTrade's point-in-time screener using filings available by August 18, 2026 and end-of-day prices on or before that date. The generated SQL was inspected. Two data cautions matter: the TSM financial row had a stale period mapping and TXG's headline year-over-year comparison was distorted by patent-settlement revenue. Primary company releases override those two fields. Brokerage option availability was checked on August 24. Every name had at least three expirations between 30 and 400 DTE.

This is a **forward universe audit** using information available in August 2026. It cannot retroactively purify or certify a backtest whose historical universe was chosen with later knowledge.

## Tradability result

Every ticker had current long-call expirations inside the strategy's 30-to-400-day fallback range. The thinnest menus were ADPT, VCYT, and TWST, each with only three or four eligible expirations. That is enough for the current resolver, although it makes bid, spread, and affordability checks more important.

| Option-menu tier | Names |
| --- | --- |
| 9 to 11 eligible expirations | MRNA, MRK, TMO, DHR, BMY, NVDA, ANET, KLAC, TSM, MRVL, LRCX |
| 5 to 8 | BNTX, RXRX, SDGR, GH, NTRA, ILMN, QGEN, TXG, A |
| 3 to 4 | ADPT, VCYT, TWST |

This verifies listed expiration availability. It does not prove that every future strike will have an acceptable bid, spread, price, or Public order eligibility. Those are still runtime gates.

## Decision and next test

- **No evidence of a disguised penny-stock universe:** every name exceeded roughly $1.6 billion of market value in the August screen and every name had an active options menu.
- **One stock fails the combined test:** RXRX is both the weakest public company and one of the weakest links to the actual therapy.
- **Two credible companies fail the mechanism test:** SDGR and BMY were removed because their Moderna-specific revenue sensitivity is limited.
- **Several retained links are intentionally broad:** TXG, TMO, DHR, and A can participate in personalized-oncology workflows, while revenue sensitivity at the whole-company level remains limited.
- **Semis are a separate regime:** they diversify the book when biotech trend breaks. They should never be marketed as direct beneficiaries of the Moderna readout.
